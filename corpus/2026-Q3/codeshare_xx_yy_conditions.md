### 1. PURPOSE

1.1 This document states which carrier's rules apply to a codeshare booking sold under the
XX/YY free-sale block-space arrangement. It is issued to reservations, ticketing, call-centre
and airport staff and is to be applied literally. Where staff cannot determine the answer from
this document, the file is to be queued to the Partnerships desk and not decided locally.

1.2 A **codeshare** booking is one in which the flight number carried on the traffic document
(the **marketing carrier** designator) is not the designator of the carrier physically
operating the aircraft (the **operating carrier**). An **interline** booking, by contrast, is
one in which each sector is both sold and operated under its own designator. The two are
frequently confused at the counter. They are not the same and the rules below differ.

### 2. FLIGHT NUMBER RANGES

2.1 The following ranges are reserved and are the fastest way to identify what a booking
actually is:

| Range | Marketing carrier | Operating carrier | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| XX 0001 – XX 2999 | XX | XX | Wholly Kraken metal |
| XX 6000 – XX 6499 | XX | YY | Codeshare — Wyvern operates |
| XX 6500 – XX 6999 | XX | Ground partner (rail/bus) | Surface segment, not carriage by air |
| YY 0001 – YY 0999 | YY | YY | Wholly Wyvern metal |
| YY 7100 – YY 7399 | YY | XX | Codeshare — Kraken operates |

2.2 Consequently **XX 1487** (IST–CDG) and **XX 2201** (IST–FCO) fall in the 0001–2999 range
and are wholly Kraken-operated services. They are **not** codeshares. **YY 88** (CDG–JFK)
falls in the YY 0001–0999 range and is a wholly Wyvern-operated service. An itinerary
combining XX 1487 with YY 88 is therefore an **interline** itinerary and is governed by
IATA-INT-XX-YY-2026-Q3, not by this document.

2.3 Staff are reminded that the presence of two designators on one itinerary does not by
itself make the itinerary a codeshare. See 1.2.

### 3. THE GOVERNING RULE — WHICH CARRIER, WHICH SUBJECT

3.1 On a codeshare sector the split is as follows and admits of no exception:

- **The operating carrier's IRROPS standard operating procedure applies.** Delay handling,
  misconnection care, meal vouchers, refreshment thresholds, hotel accommodation, ground
  transport and the sequence of reaccommodation are all determined by the procedure of the
  carrier whose crew and aircraft are performing the flight. A passenger sitting in an
  Wyvern Overseas cabin on an XX 6xxx flight number is cared for under Wyvern's procedure,
  not Kraken's, regardless of what the boarding pass says.

- **The marketing carrier's fare rules govern penalties.** Change penalty, cancellation
  penalty, refundability, no-show treatment, advance purchase, minimum stay and the fare
  family conditions (LITE, CLASSIC, FLEX) are those filed by the carrier whose designator
  appears on the traffic document. A passenger holding an XX 6xxx coupon in booking class K
  is priced under the Kraken CLASSIC fare rule sheet even though Wyvern flew the aircraft.

3.2 The consequence, stated plainly because it is repeatedly got wrong: **on a single
codeshare sector the care owed to the passenger and the money charged to the passenger can be
determined by two different carriers' rule books at the same time.** This is intended and is
not a filing error.

3.3 Where the operating carrier's SOP entitles a passenger to care and the marketing carrier's
fare rule simultaneously levies a penalty, both apply. The care is not set off against the
penalty and the penalty is not waived by the provision of care. Set-off requires an express
waiver from the marketing carrier's revenue desk.

### 4. AMBIGUOUS CASES REFERRED TO THE PARTNERSHIPS DESK

4.1 The following are known ambiguity generators and must **not** be decided at the counter:

(a) A codeshare sector where the operating carrier has itself subcontracted the flight to a
    third carrier under a wet lease. Whose IRROPS SOP applies is not settled by this document.
(b) An itinerary mixing a codeshare sector and an interline sector, where the misconnect
    occurs at the junction between the two.
(c) A voluntary change requested during an irregular operation, where it is unclear whether
    the change is caused by the irregularity (no penalty, reaccommodation) or is voluntary
    (marketing carrier's penalty applies under 3.1).
(d) A corporate or staff-travel booking on a codeshare sector, where an internal Kraken
    travel policy appears to grant a free change that the filed fare rule does not.

4.2 Case (d) in particular is not a licence to waive a filed penalty. Filed fare rules are
lodged with the distribution systems; internal policy documents are not. A discrepancy between
them is resolved by the Partnerships desk and, if necessary, by Revenue Management.

### 5. DISPLAY, DISCLOSURE AND CODE OBLIGATIONS

5.1 The operating carrier must be disclosed at the point of sale, on the itinerary receipt and
in any schedule display, in the form "operated by Wyvern Overseas Airways" or "operated by
Kraken Air" as the case may be. 5.2 The disclosure obligation is regulatory and is not waived
by the passenger's familiarity with the route. 5.3 Codeshare flight numbers are not to be
quoted to a passenger as the operating flight number when arranging airport meeting
assistance, since the airport operates against the operating desginator.

### 6. BAGGAGE AND CHECK-IN ON CODESHARE SECTORS

6.1 Check-in is performed by the operating carrier or its ground handling agent. 6.2 Baggage
allowance is determined under Resolution 302 as set out in Clause 2 of the Interline Traffic
Agreement and is not altered by the codeshare arrangement. 6.3 Excess baggage charges are
collected by the operating carrier at the operating carrier's published rate and are retained
by the operating carrier.
